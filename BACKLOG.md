- `first-glance-clarity: intentTier--danger vs base color converge under red-green color-vision deficiency` — new finding while re-verifying the above fix. Simulated deuteranopia/protanopia (standard RGB approximation matrices) on the three intent colors: base attack `#ffb74d` and danger `#ff6e40` are both amber/orange and land close together after simulation (Euclidean distance ~46 in simulated RGB) versus their distance from safe `#80d8ff` (~169-179) — i.e. `guard`(safe/blue) stays clearly distinct, but a colorblind player relying on color alone would have a much harder time telling "boss is charging/about to hit twice as hard" (danger) apart from "boss is about to do a normal attack" (base/no tint) at a glance, which is exactly the higher-stakes distinction this feature exists to telegraph. The text label (`次のターン: 溜めている(次は2倍ダメージ)` vs `次の攻撃: Nダメージ`) still fully disambiguates so no information is lost, just the fast glance-parse color shortcut is weak for this specific pair. Consider a secondary non-color cue for the danger tier specifically (e.g. bold weight, an icon/prefix like "⚠", or the existing pulse animation replaying periodically while charge is active) so color-vision-deficient players get the same at-a-glance urgency signal sighted players do.

- `beat:boss-telegraph mechanism:guard-pitch-contour` — low-priority nuance flagged by `game-dopamine-evaluator`'s qualitative pass, evaluated here from a presentation-correctness angle and considered worth tracking (not urgent): the `guard` cue's two-tone sequence is descending (262Hz → 196Hz), which in typical game-audio convention reads as "negative/fail" (e.g. a miss or death stinger — this game's own `playEndGameSting(false)` uses a descending sawtooth triple for death), while ascending tones read as "positive/success" (the win sting is ascending). `guard` means "safe, zero incoming damage this turn" — the opposite of what a descending contour conventionally signals — so on a player's *first* exposures (before they've learned by repetition that low+descending=guard=harmless here), the pitch direction alone could momentarily misread as bad news. Confirmed the color channel (`.intentAlert--safe` blue `#80d8ff` vs `.intentAlert--danger` red/orange `#ff6e40`) already disambiguates unambiguously and passes WCAG AA per `game-usability-tester`'s check, so this is not a blocker — just a case where the audio-alone signal fights the color-alone signal on convention. Suggested (cheap, no new dependencies): flip `guard`'s tone order to ascending (e.g. 196Hz→262Hz, same frequencies, reversed order) so both channels agree in the "safe" direction; verify `charge`'s existing 660→880 ascending order still reads as danger via its color tint doing the disambiguating work there instead (rising pitch is more ambiguous for danger than for safety, but charge already leans on its red tint + higher absolute pitch + square-wave harshness, so it's lower-risk as-is).
